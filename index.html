Gap #2 — Case Study

Feed Strategy — A gating strategy built on identity.

WATT Global Media's Feed Strategy is one of the leading publications in animal nutrition. In 2024, they implemented a gating strategy designed around one principle: match the gate to the content, and never gate someone you already know.

📄

Full Gate on Articles

Core editorial content — the reason readers come to the site. High-value content justifies the ask.

73%

of registrations

📊

Metered Gate on Other Content

Lower friction where appropriate. Free views before the gate triggers. Different content, different strategy.

27%

of registrations

✅

Known Users? No Gate.

Newsletter subscribers and identified users pass through seamlessly. If you already know who they are, why ask again?

0

friction for known readers

That's identity-first gating. The gate only appears for the audience you don't know yet.

Gap #2 — Your options

Gating isn't all or nothing. Pick your strategy.

Metered
X free articles, then gate.
Lowest friction.
Selective
Gate premium content or
specific sections only.
Full
All content requires
identification.

Most publishers use combinations. The point is to have a strategy, not to gate everything or nothing.

Gap #2 — The key differentiator

Control the experience. Not a popup in sight.

When gating is built into the content layer, registration becomes part of the reading experience. Native, not bolted on.

Anonymous → email validated → name, title, company → newsletter subscriber → fully profiled. One interaction at a time.

Your action plan

Three things to do Monday morning.

1

Audit your known audience percentage.

Pull your analytics. What percentage of last month's traffic can you identify by name and email? If you don't know the number, that's your first problem.

2

Visit your own site in an incognito window.

Go through the registration process as a new reader. Count the popups. Count the form fields. Then do it again as a returning user. Are you asking for data you already have?

3

Build one advertiser report that leads with WHO, not HOW MANY.

Before your next renewal, show titles, companies, and engagement depth — not just impressions served. If you can't build that report today, you know what needs to change.
